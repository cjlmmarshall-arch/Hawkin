# Nature reading list — January to August 2026

**A note on "articles I might like":** there was no reading history, saved-paper list, or interest profile available to work from, so the selection is deliberately broad rather than personalised. The categories below are my own; they're chosen to span *Nature*'s main beats so you can tell me which two or three actually interest you, and I'll re-cut the list against those.

## January 2026 — vol. 649, issues 8095–8099

### 🌍 Climate & planet
- **[Emerging climate impact on carbon sinks in a consolidated carbon budget](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09802-5)** *(1 Jan, issue 8095)* — The global carbon budget has never quite balanced: the mean estimates of sources and sinks didn't match the observed rise in atmospheric CO₂. This reconciles it. The natural land sink turns out to be substantially *smaller* than thought (process-based models overestimated it by ~23% for 2012–2021), while emissions from land-use change are revised *up* by ~14%. Closing the imbalance by up to 25% matters for anyone tracking whether climate policy is working. Accompanied by the explainer **[Revised estimates of CO₂ sources and sinks improve global carbon accounting](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-03981-x)**.
- **A database of 2.75 billion buildings** *(1 Jan, issue 8095)* — A global building-stock dataset pitched as a tool for monitoring urban planning, climate change, disaster risk — and, intriguingly, corruption.

### 🧠 Brain & mind
- **[Mapping the genetic landscape across 14 psychiatric disorders](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09820-3)** *(8 Jan, issue 8096)* — Genomic analysis across 14 childhood- and adult-onset psychiatric disorders finds **five** underlying genomic factors — Compulsive, Schizophrenia–bipolar, Neurodevelopmental, Internalizing, and Substance-use — that account for roughly 66% of the genetic variance of the individual disorders on average. Pleiotropic variants point to early neurodevelopmental processes. This is the kind of paper that quietly redraws diagnostic categories.
- **[Adenosine signalling drives antidepressant actions of ketamine and ECT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09755-9)** *(8 Jan, issue 8096)* — Two very different fast-acting antidepressants turn out to share one mechanism. Both trigger adenosine surges in the medial prefrontal cortex and hippocampus; knock out A1/A2A receptors and the therapeutic effect disappears. New ketamine analogues built on this show better efficacy with fewer side effects. Arguably the most clinically consequential paper of the month.
- **All-optical mapping of neural connectivity in motor cortex** *(8 Jan, issue 8096)* — Links network topology to the dynamics and computation underlying naturalistic goal-directed behaviour.

### 🌌 Cosmos & fundamental physics
- **[Little red dots as young supermassive black holes in dense ionized cocoons](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09900-4)** *(15 Jan, issue 8097 — cover)* — JWST's "little red dots" have been argued over since 2022: star-packed early galaxies, or improbably massive black holes? The highest-quality spectra yet settle it toward young supermassive black holes that are much *smaller* than assumed, with their broad spectral lines produced by electron scattering in a dense ionized cocoon rather than Doppler motion. A satisfying resolution to a genuinely weird observation.
- **Direct observation of the Migdal effect in neutron–nucleus collisions**, and **coherent nonlinear four-photon interactions with core–shell electrons** using X-ray pulses *(15 Jan, issue 8097)*.

### 🧬 Genomes, cells & ageing
- **Variant-effect prediction** *(29 Jan, issue 8099)* — The issue's theme: pinning down the precise functional consequences of individual DNA sequence changes, the bottleneck standing between genome-wide association hits and actual biology.

### 🏛️ Science & society
- **One year of the second Trump administration** *(22 Jan, issue 8098)* — *Nature*'s accounting of twelve months of grant cuts and staff reductions across US science agencies.

## February 2026 — vol. 650, issues 8100–8103

### 🌌 Cosmos & fundamental physics
- **Dark Energy Survey: six-year results** *(5 Feb, issue 8100)* — The full DES dataset sharpens rather than resolves the tensions in ΛCDM, adding to the tentative-but-accumulating case that dark energy may evolve with time instead of being a constant. If that holds up it is the biggest story in cosmology this decade.

### 🌍 Climate & planet
- **[Pesticide residues alter taxonomic and functional biodiversity in soils](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09991-z)** *(12 Feb, issue 8101)* — 63 pesticides measured across 373 sites in 26 European countries. Residues were present at **70%** of sites and were the second-strongest driver of soil biodiversity patterns after soil properties themselves — suppressing arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi and bacterivore nematodes, and perturbing nitrogen and phosphorus cycling. See also **[Pesticide cocktails negatively affect soil biodiversity](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00039-4)**.
- **Field pennycress as a winter crop** *(5 Feb, issue 8100)* — A rare climate story where the carbon-storage case and the farm-profitability case point the same way.
- **A fresh effort to explain "dark oxygen" on the Pacific seafloor** *(5 Feb, issue 8100)* — Following up the contested claim that polymetallic nodules produce oxygen in the abyss.

### 🏺 Deep past & the human story
- **[Rock art from at least 67,800 years ago in Sulawesi](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09968-y)** *(19 Feb, issue 8102)* — Laser-ablation U-series dating of calcite over a hand stencil at Liang Metanduno on Muna Island gives 71.6 ± 3.8 ka for the overlying crust, so ≥67.8 ka for the art beneath. That beats the previous Sulawesi record by 16,600 years and edges past the Neanderthal-attributed Spanish stencils — making it the oldest securely dated parietal art attributable to our species, and strengthening the northern route into Sahul. Popular write-up: **[Hand stencils in Indonesian cave are world's oldest known artworks](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00018-9)**.
- **"Blocking games" played in Europe centuries earlier than realised** *(19 Feb, issue 8102)*.

### 🦋 Life on Earth
- **A virus integrated into wasp genomes that subdues moth larvae** *(19 Feb, issue 8102)* — Domesticated virus as a weapon system; one of evolution's better plot twists.

### 💊 Medicine & public health
- **Breath metabolites correlate with gut-microbe populations** *(5 Feb, issue 8100)* — In mice and humans; a possible non-invasive diagnostic route.
- **Engineered *E. coli* for sustainable synthesis** *(5 Feb, issue 8100)* — More sustainable routes to new drugs and other chemicals.
- **Organoids for testing spinal-cord damage treatments** *(26 Feb, issue 8103)* — 3D cell structures as a testbed for paralysis therapies.

### 🤖 AI & the machinery of science
- **A 3D-printed robot with adjustable legs and joints** that can switch from "elbows" to "knees" *(19 Feb, issue 8102)*, and **a gecko-footpad-inspired adhesive** letting a robot climb both smooth and rough vertical surfaces *(26 Feb, issue 8103)*.

## March 2026 — vol. 651, issues 8104–8107

### 🤖 AI & the machinery of science
- **[Towards end-to-end automation of AI research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10265-5)** *(26 Mar, issue 8107)* — **The month's most-discussed paper.** An AI system generates research ideas, writes code, runs experiments, analyses data, writes the manuscript and reviews itself. Three of its papers went to a machine-learning conference workshop; one scored high enough for acceptance (the workshop's acceptance rate was 70%, and the accepted paper reported a negative result — read the fine print). Background: **[How to build an AI scientist: first peer-reviewed paper spills the secrets](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00899-w)**; *Nature*'s editorial line: **[AI scientists are changing research — institutions, funders and publishers must respond](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00934-w)**.
- **[A 'virtual cell' captures the most basic process of life: bacterial division](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00786-4)** *(19 Mar, issue 8106)* — A 3D simulation of DNA replication, division and nearly every chemical reaction in a bacterial cell. Not fully faithful — unknown-function genes get placeholders — but it's the first time a cell has been watched growing and reproducing *in silico* at molecular resolution. (*Nature*'s coverage; the underlying paper is in *Cell*.)

### 🌌 Cosmos & fundamental physics
- **[Lense–Thirring precessing magnetar engine drives a superluminous supernova](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10151-0)** *(12 Mar, issue 8105)* — Superluminous supernovae are ≥10× brighter than ordinary ones and nobody could prove what powers them. Six months of near-continuous global monitoring of SN 2024afav caught oscillating light-curve bumps revealing a rapidly rotating magnetar at the centre, ringed by an infalling accretion disk that *wobbles* through Lense–Thirring precession — frame-dragging, straight out of general relativity. See **[Ultra-bright supernova wobbles like a spinning top](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00490-3)**.
- **Why sticky tape screeches** *(5 Mar, issue 8104)* — Weak shock waves triggered by micro-cracks as the tape unspools. A small, delightful piece of everyday physics.

### 🧬 Genomes, cells & ageing
- **A protein that reinforces the blood–brain barrier** *(5 Mar, issue 8104)* — The barrier grows leaky with age; this identifies something that shores it up.

### 💊 Medicine & public health
- **KLHL6 as a dual-negative regulator of T cell exhaustion and mitochondrial dysfunction** *(12 Mar, issue 8105)* — A plausible target for boosting anti-tumour immunity.
- **Lymphoedema improved by clearing cholesterol deposits** *(12 Mar, issue 8105)* — Removing the deposits promoted lymphatic vessel regeneration in mice.

### 🏺 Deep past & the human story
- **Plugging gaps in the pre-Devonian fossil record** *(5 Mar, issue 8104)* — Min Zhu and colleagues, across several papers.
- **Ancient DNA from feathers in Peru** *(19 Mar, issue 8106)* — Suggests the Ychsma culture imported birds from the distant Amazon.

### 🦋 Life on Earth
- **Ant queens that invade** *(5 Mar, issue 8104)* — *Temnothorax kinomurai* queens survive by taking over the nests of related species and co-opting their workers.

### 🏛️ Science & society
- **[Major conference catches illicit AI use — and rejects hundreds of papers](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00893-2)** — The other side of the AI-in-research coin, in the same news cycle as the AI-scientist paper.

## April 2026 — vol. 652, issues 8108–8112

### 🌌 Cosmos & fundamental physics
- **[Antimatter has been transported for the first time ever — in the back of CERN's truck](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00950-w)** *(2 Apr, issue 8108)* — 92 antiprotons in a superconducting-magnet bottle at 4 K under hard vacuum, driven half an hour around CERN's Meyrin site, running autonomously without external power for four hours and losing nothing. It sounds like a stunt; it's the enabling step for doing precision antimatter physics in quiet labs far from the antimatter factory. Builds on the 2025 proof-of-concept that moved ordinary protons the same way.
- **Extreme field generation via a coherent harmonic focus** *(30 Apr, issue 8112)* — Tailoring relativistic laser–plasma interactions on femtosecond timescales.

### 🧬 Genomes, cells & ageing
- **[Thymic health consequences in adults](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10242-y)** and **[Thymic health and immunotherapy outcomes in patients with cancer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10243-x)** *(23 Apr, issue 8111)* — **A standout pair.** The thymus was written off as vestigial in adults. A deep-learning framework scoring thymic health from routine radiographs, applied to the National Lung Screening Trial (n = 25,031) and Framingham (n = 2,581), finds it predicts longevity and age-associated disease risk — and in non-small-cell lung cancer, higher thymic health tracks with reduced progression and all-cause mortality. Read with the feature **[Could this mysterious disappearing organ hold the key to longevity?](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02149-5)** and **[Thymus health is a predictor of lifelong well-being and immunotherapy effectiveness](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00633-6)**.
- **A review of de novo protein design** *(30 Apr, issue 8112)* — Methodological advances, current limits, and where designed proteins go next. A good orientation piece if you've watched this field from outside.
- **Cell-type- and isoform-specific translation in the hippocampus** *(23 Apr, issue 8111)* — Post-transcriptional control across neuronal and non-neuronal cell types.

### 🌍 Climate & planet
- **The human light footprint** *(9 Apr, issue 8109)* — Light pollution isn't a monotonic brightening: brightening and dimming happen simultaneously in different places, and both are intensifying.

### 🦋 Life on Earth
- **Microbial genomes from 820 reef-building corals across 99 Pacific reefs** *(16 Apr, issue 8110)* — Reframes coral reefs as reservoirs of molecular diversity worth conserving for what's *in* them, not just the coral.
- **Restored forests reach 75% similarity to old growth within 30 years** *(30 Apr, issue 8112)* — Encouraging, with the caveat that full recovery still takes several decades more.

## May 2026 — vol. 653, issues 8113–8116

### 🧬 Genomes, cells & ageing
- **[The evolutionary history and unique genetic diversity of Indigenous Americans](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10406-w)** *(7 May, issue 8113)* — The Indigenous American Genomic Diversity Project: 128 new high-coverage whole genomes spanning 45 populations, 28 language families and 8 countries (Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, Mexico, Paraguay, Peru), combined with ancient genomes. Reveals extensive previously uncharacterised diversity and at least three dispersals into South America followed by long-term regional continuity.
- **Ancient mitochondria form specialised 'sacs'** *(7 May, issue 8113)* — By shedding their outer membranes; a genuinely odd bit of cell biology.

### 🌍 Climate & planet
- **[Rising global hail damage potential in a warming world](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10543-2)** *(28 May, issue 8116)* — Warmer, wetter low-level air makes bigger hailstones: ≥30 mm stones rise 37.9–51.8% in frequency while smaller ones decline, driving a **36.5–42.1% increase in global hail damage potential** by late century depending on emissions. Mid-latitudes get hit hardest; tropics and subtropics see reductions. An underrated climate-impact channel with direct insurance and agriculture consequences. See **[Hailstorms are predicted to hit harder with climate change](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01639-w)**.
- **The first high-elevation fire in central African mountains in 12,000 years** *(21 May, issue 8115)* — A twenty-first-century first, in a region that hadn't burned throughout the Holocene.

### 💊 Medicine & public health
- **A biomarker predicting immunotherapy response in certain breast cancers** *(7 May, issue 8113)*.
- **How heart pressure affects cancer cell multiplication** *(7 May, issue 8113)* — In mouse hearts; mechanical environment as a factor in tumour growth.
- **Blood-cancer remission using T cells that behave like stem cells** *(14 May, issue 8114)*, plus **a vaccine in development for a rare rodent virus**.

### 🦋 Life on Earth
- **Higher-order interactions drive tree species diversity decline** *(14 May, issue 8114)* — Species don't just interact pairwise, and modelling that changes the diversity picture.

### 🏛️ Science & society
- **Racially diverse graduating classes and starting salaries** *(21 May, issue 8115)* — Higher racial diversity among classmates is associated with higher median starting salaries after graduation.

## June 2026 — vol. 654, issues 8117–8120

### 🧬 Genomes, cells & ageing
- **[Universal transcriptomic hallmarks of mammalian ageing and mortality](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10542-3)** *(4 Jun, issue 8117)* — **My pick of the month.** Over 11,000 transcriptomes, 25+ tissues, four mammals (mouse, rat, macaque, human) yield "transcriptomic clocks" that estimate not just chronological age but expected mortality, time to death, chronic disease risk and the effect of lifespan-modifying interventions. Ageing signatures — including CDKN1A and LGALS3 — are conserved across species and cell types, and the protein levels track mortality and multimorbidity in UK Biobank. Crucially the readouts group into functional gene modules, so ageing becomes quantifiable pathway by pathway. Coverage: **[Gene-expression patterns can be used to estimate mortality risk and chronological age](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01326-w)**.
- **[Move over, AlphaFold: open-source model predicts shape of 1 billion proteins](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01686-3)** *(4 Jun, issue 8117)* — ESMFold2's ESM Atlas: 1.1 billion predicted structures plus billions more sequences, exceeding the AlphaFold Database by over 800 million entries — and open source, which is the real story.

### 🌌 Cosmos & fundamental physics
- **[Measurement of reactor neutrino oscillation with the first JUNO data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10538-z)** *(11 Jun, issue 8118)* — 20,000 tonnes of liquid scintillator, 700 m underground in Guangdong, and just **59 days** of data: sin²θ₁₂ = 0.3092 ± 0.0087 and Δm²₂₁ = (7.50 ± 0.12) × 10⁻⁵ eV², cutting uncertainties by a factor of 1.6 versus *all* previous experiments combined. A remarkable debut, and the mass-ordering measurement is now clearly in reach. See **[JUNO experiment ushers in next generation of neutrino experiments](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01585-7)**.

### 🤖 AI & the machinery of science
- **[Humans outperform AI at this highly rigorous mathematics test](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01888-9)** *(25 Jun, issue 8120)* — A benchmark of previously unseen problems where AI systems still fall short of top mathematicians. Useful counterweight to March's AI-scientist paper.
- **[Public use of a generalist LLM chatbot for health queries](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44360-026-00117-x)** *(18 Jun, issue 8119; *Nature Health*)* — 500,000 real Copilot conversations from January 2026, sorted into a 12-category intent taxonomy. Personal health intents dominate — symptom assessment, condition management, emotional well-being — and a substantial share is people asking on behalf of someone they care for. Framed as evidence of gaps in healthcare provision and of what AI companies now owe their users. *Nature*'s take: **[People are turning to AI chatbots to plug gaps in health information](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01737-9)**.

### 💊 Medicine & public health
- **[Electronic cigarette use after smoking cessation and lung cancer risk](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04469-5)** *(18 Jun, issue 8119; *Nature Medicine*)* — 4.5 million Korean adults, 24+ million person-years. Versus complete quitters, vaping after quitting carried a higher lung cancer incidence (aHR 1.56, 95% CI 1.24–1.97) and mortality — i.e. e-cigarettes appear to attenuate the benefit of stopping. Retrospective and observational, so read the caveats, but it lands hard on a live policy question. Coverage: **[Huge study hints at risks of switching from tobacco cigarettes to vapes](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01834-9)**.

### 🦋 Life on Earth
- **The sugarcane POJ2878 genome** *(25 Jun, issue 8120)* — 118 chromosomes resolved, clarifying the genetic architecture behind biomass productivity and sugar yield in a notoriously messy polyploid genome.

## July 2026 — vol. 655, issues 8121–8125

### 🤖 AI & the machinery of science
- **Two multi-agent AI systems for running the research cycle** *(9 Jul, issue 8122)* — Independent teams, same ambition: generate hypotheses, propose experiments, interpret results, refine. Google DeepMind's **Co-Scientist**, built on Gemini 2.0, was pointed at drug candidates for acute myeloid leukaemia. Pair this with March's end-to-end automation paper and June's maths benchmark for the full arc of the year's argument about what AI can and can't do in science.

### 🌍 Climate & planet
- **[Global high-resolution mapping of seagrass to support conservation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10704-3)** *(16 Jul, issue 8123)* — 4.75 million satellite images across 2019–20 and 2023–24, mapping meadows to 30 m depth: **148,506 km²** of seagrass, 69% of it concentrated in just five countries (Bahamas, Cuba, USA, Australia, Indonesia) — and only **21%** inside marine protected areas. 4% was lost and another 4% degraded in four years. A blue-carbon baseline that didn't previously exist.
- **An alga behind the deaths of more than a million marine animals** *(16 Jul, issue 8123)*.

### 🌌 Cosmos & fundamental physics

- **An "ordinary space rock" with a comet-like tail** *(23 Jul, issue 8124)*.

### 💊 Medicine & public health

- **GDF15 and dementia risk** *(9 Jul, issue 8122)* — People under 55 with relatively high levels of the molecule had elevated dementia risk.

### 🧠 Brain & mind

## August 2026 (to 13 Aug) — vol. 656, issues 8126–8127

### 🧠 Brain & mind
- **[Cortical development dynamics across autism spectrum disorder mouse models](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10679-1)** *(6 Aug, issue 8126)* — 251 samples from 11 monogenic ASD mouse models, single-nucleus multi-omics across three developmental stages, both sexes, two brain regions. Despite genetic heterogeneity, the mutations **converge** on the radial glial cell lineage — and the disruption is a transient developmental delay that resolves postnatally rather than permanent misspecification. The largest transcriptional differences appear later, in early postnatal neurons. A genuinely reframing result, with sex-specific effects throughout.
- **Microglia reaching out to neurons after ketamine** *(13 Aug, issue 8127)* — In female mice; a nice companion to January's adenosine paper.

### 🤖 AI & the machinery of science
- **[Universal cell embedding provides a foundation model for cell biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10689-z)** *(6 Aug, issue 8126)* — Self-supervised training on 36 million cells from hundreds of experiments, dozens of tissues and eight species produces a unified latent space; new cells can be embedded with **no labelling, training or fine-tuning**. The accompanying Integrated Mega-scale Atlas covers 1,000+ named cell types.
- **AI diagnostic models are vulnerable to membership inference attacks** *(6 Aug, issue 8126)* — i.e. you can determine whether a specific patient's data was in the training set. A serious problem for medical AI deployment, and the kind of finding that should shape regulation.

### 🌍 Climate & planet
- **60 years of ocean iron fertilisation, modelled across ten regions** *(6 Aug, issue 8126)* — Identifies the Pacific and Southern Oceans as the most efficient areas for carbon removal. Relevant to a geoengineering debate that is about to get much louder.

### 🦋 Life on Earth
- **The RGF peptide axis controls cold resilience in tomato and rice** *(6 Aug, issue 8126)* — Modulating it prevents cold-induced yield losses; a direct lever on food security under climate volatility.
- **Queen mole rats suppress reproduction chemically** *(13 Aug, issue 8127)* — Isopropyl myristate is the signal enforcing the reproductive hierarchy. Decades of naked mole-rat behaviour, finally with a molecule attached.

## Threads worth following across the year

- **Can AI do science?** March's automation paper → June's maths benchmark (humans still ahead) → July's two multi-agent lab systems → the parallel policy fight over undisclosed AI use in peer review. *Nature* has been running the case for and against in the same pages all year.
- **AI as biological instrument.** ESMFold2's billion structures (Jun) → the universal cell embedding model (Aug) → the whole-cell simulation (Mar). Foundation models are becoming the default substrate for biology.
- **Ageing as a measurable quantity.** Transcriptomic clocks (Jun), thymic health scoring (Apr), the blood–brain barrier protein (Mar).
- **Carbon accounting under revision.** The consolidated carbon budget (Jan) → seagrass baselines (Jul) → iron fertilisation efficiency (Aug).

## How this list was built, and its limits

- **Coverage:** every weekly issue from vol. 649 no. 8095 (1 Jan 2026) through vol. 656 no. 8127 (13 Aug 2026).
- **Sourcing:** direct access to `nature.com` and to the Crossref/OpenAlex metadata APIs was blocked by this environment's network egress policy, so items were gathered through web search against `nature.com` and cross-checked against issue-level tables of contents. Every linked item was confirmed to exist; items listed **without** a link are ones where the issue highlight was verified but a direct article URL wasn't recoverable under that constraint.
- **Journal scope:** entries are from *Nature* itself unless noted. A few are flagged as *Nature Medicine*, *Nature Health*, or as *Nature* news coverage of a paper published elsewhere (the March virtual-cell work appeared in *Cell*).
- **Dating:** items are grouped by **print issue date**. Some papers carry a 2025 DOI stem (`s41586-025-…`) because they appeared online in late 2025 before print publication in a January or February 2026 issue.
